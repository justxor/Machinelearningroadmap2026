# 🎤 ML interview prep

A systematic prep for Junior/Middle/Senior interviews for ML / Data Scientist / LLM Engineer / MLOps Engineer roles. No cramming — understanding.

## 🎯 Goal of this module

Give you a **working base** to pass the 4–6 stages of a typical ML interview loop: technical screen, ML theory, ML system design, coding, behavioral. Every topic comes with questions and full answers, so that you do not "memorize wording" but actually understand and can defend your answer.

## 🗓️ A 4-week prep plan

If you have 4 weeks until the interview and you already work in ML, this plan is enough. If you have no experience, add another 4–8 weeks for the base from the main courses in this repo.

**Week 1 — technical base:**
- Read [01-ml-classic.md](./01-ml-classic.md) and [02-deep-learning.md](./02-deep-learning.md).
- Answer every question out loud. Wherever you wobble, open the matching lesson in [neural-networks/](../neural-networks/README.md) or [data-science/](../data-science/README.md).
- 5 LeetCode mediums per day.

**Week 2 — specialization:**
- Read [03-llm-rag-agents.md](./03-llm-rag-agents.md) (if you are interviewing for LLM/GenAI).
- Grind 50 SQL problems on [StrataScratch](https://stratascratch.com).
- Read about real systems in your domain (target company + papers).

**Week 3 — system design:**
- Read [04-system-design.md](./04-system-design.md).
- On a whiteboard or Miro, do 5 cases on your own: search, recommendations, fraud, chatbot, moderation.
- Find a partner for a mock interview.

**Week 4 — final stretch:**
- Read [06-behavioral.md](./06-behavioral.md), prepare 8–10 STAR stories.
- Run 3 mock interviews (with friends or paid via [interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io)).
- Rest the day before the interview. Sleep well.

## 🧠 Prep principles

1. **Understanding > memorization.** If you can explain a topic to a 12-year-old, you know it. If you only recite a textbook definition, you do not.
2. **Think out loud.** The interviewer scores your thought process, not just the final answer. Silence = fail.
3. **Do not be afraid to say "I don't know".** Honestly admitting a gap and offering how you would figure it out beats making things up.
4. **Clarify the question.** "What context? What constraints? What data?" — half the score in system design.
5. **Show trade-offs.** Not "X is better than Y", but "X wins on A, loses on B; in our context A matters more".
6. **Tie answers to experience.** "I did something similar on project X, Y worked" — instantly raises trust.

## 💰 What is asked at each level

| Level | Interview focus | How long to prep |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Junior** | Coding + ML basics. Less system design, more "do you know the fundamentals" | 2–4 weeks |
| **Middle** | Equal weight: coding, ML/DL, light system design, projects | 4–6 weeks |
| **Senior** | System design, leadership, architecture. Coding still present, checks clarity of thought | 6–8 weeks |
| **Staff/Principal** | Strategy, impact, culture. ML at the "I can interview anyone" level | 8–12 weeks + experience |

## ❓ What to do after a rejection

Rejections are normal. Top engineers get 1 offer out of every 5–10 interviews.

1. **Ask for feedback.** Not always given, but sometimes yes. Specifics are gold.
2. **Track your weak spots.** If you fail system design three times — that is a signal.
3. **Do not take it personally.** Sometimes it has nothing to do with you and everything to do with company internals.
4. **Do not go to the next interview immediately.** Three days of rest beats a burned-out loop.
5. **Watch your funnel.** 50 applications → 10 interviews → 1 offer = normal for a junior. Lower application-to-interview rate? Improve your resume.
